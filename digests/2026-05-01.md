## Emerging Themes

- **Capital Concentration in Legal AI:** Harvey ($11B) and Legora ($5.55B) together absorbed nearly half of all Q1 2026 legal tech funding globally. The funding gap between these two and the rest of the market is widening, likely forcing consolidation or niche specialisation among second-tier vendors.

- **Foundation Model Providers as Direct Competitors:** Anthropic's move into legal tech means AI model suppliers may now compete with the platforms (like Harvey) built on top of their models. This creates strategic tension and potential pricing/access risks for any product dependent on a single model provider.

- **Accuracy vs. Scale — A Reckoning:** CoCounsel's hallucination incident, despite reaching 1 million users, signals that scale and brand recognition do not guarantee reliability. The market is beginning to demand verifiable accuracy benchmarks, particularly for Australian regulatory and case law contexts.

- **UK Government AI Mandate as Market Catalyst:** £4.5M in LawtechUK funding and plans for AI in UK courts will accelerate institutional adoption timelines and set regulatory norms that may flow into Australian policy (Australia often follows UK legal tech regulatory direction with a 12–18 month lag).

- **Australian Market Consolidation Risk:** Actionstep's wind-down of FilePro and LawMaster is forcing thousands of Australian law firms to migrate platforms. This is a rare displacement event — a significant acquisition and integration opportunity for any player capable of offering a credible alternative or migration path.

- **Practitioner Bypass of Vertical Legal AI:** A measurable segment of lawyers is choosing to customise general-purpose AI (Claude, GPT-4) rather than pay for specialist platforms, citing cost, flexibility, and control. This pressures the mid-market positioning of vertical tools that cannot demonstrate clear superiority over well-prompted general models.
