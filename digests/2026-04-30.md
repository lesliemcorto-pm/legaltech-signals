## Emerging Themes
- **AI Capital Concentration:** Harvey and Legora together absorbed nearly half of all Q1 2026 legal tech funding, accelerating a two-tier market where pure-play AI platforms attract institutional (and now strategic) capital — Nvidia's entry into Legora signals hardware/infrastructure players betting on legal AI as a durable vertical.
- **Microsoft as Existential Threat:** Microsoft's launch of a native Legal Agent in Word — built by genuine legal tech engineers — could commoditise entry-level AI drafting, forcing specialist vendors to move upmarket into complex reasoning, agentic workflows, and deep system integration to justify premium pricing.
- **Hallucination Risk as a Buying Criterion:** The CoCounsel incident reinforces that AI reliability, not just capability, is becoming a primary differentiator; firms will increasingly demand citation grounding, audit trails, and documented hallucination safeguards as standard procurement requirements.
- **European Legal AI Battle Escalating:** Harvey's Dublin and Paris expansion puts it in direct competition with Legora on its home turf, while Legora's Nvidia backing and $100M ARR milestone signal it is not ceding ground — the resulting pressure on pricing and product differentiation will ripple through global markets including Australia.
- **Client Trust Gap:** Survey data reveals a persistent gap between client acceptance of digital tools and comfort with AI — particularly for administrative and case management work — pointing to a market need for "AI with guardrails" positioning that addresses client transparency and oversight concerns.
