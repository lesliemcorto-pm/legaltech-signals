## Emerging Themes

- **Anthropic as Legal Infrastructure Layer:** Claude for Legal's launch repositions Anthropic from API provider to platform owner inside the legal vertical. Existing legal AI platforms (Harvey, Legora, CoCounsel) simultaneously partner with and compete against the model provider — a structural tension that will define the next 12 months.

- **Open-Source Disruption of Proprietary Legal AI Moats:** "Mike" — a fully functional Harvey/Legora clone built in two weeks — is the clearest signal yet that technology is not the sustainable moat in legal AI. Distribution, trust, integrations, and enterprise relationships are what multi-billion valuations are actually pricing.

- **Harvey vs. Legora: The Regional Land Grab:** With $750M raised between them in Q1 2026, Harvey (US-dominant) and Legora (Europe-dominant) are crossing into each other's territories. Australian and global firms should expect aggressive sales campaigns from both in H2 2026.

- **Australian Forced-Migration Window:** Actionstep's wind-down of FilePro and LawMaster is a rare, large-scale forced switching event in the Australian mid-market. Competitors have 12–18 months to capture displaced firms before they settle into new platforms.

- **UK Client Trust Gap:** Survey evidence of client discomfort with AI, combined with Avvoka's scale-up and Legl's mid-market rollouts, suggests the UK market is entering a maturity phase focused on governance, transparency, and client communication — not just AI capability.

- **Fintech-Legal Convergence:** Carta's acquisition of ALSP Avantia to launch an AI-first law firm is one of several signals that non-legal technology companies are moving into legal services delivery, threatening traditional law firm and ALSP business models.
