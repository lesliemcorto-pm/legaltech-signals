## Emerging Themes
- **Platform Giants vs. Specialists:** OpenAI's entry (June 1) completes a "big three" alongside Anthropic and Microsoft — all now fielding legal-specific tools and integration ecosystems. Specialist vendors must articulate a clear moat beyond model access; distribution, vertical depth, and workflow lock-in are the credible answers.
- **Token Cost Pressure:** Rising LLM costs are creating a quiet margin crisis across legal AI, particularly for agentic and document-heavy workflows. Watch for pricing model changes, a shift toward model arbitrage, or renewed interest in on-premises/private-cloud deployment in regulated markets like Australia.
- **AI-Authorised Law Practice:** Superlegal's Utah launch is the first operational, legally authorised AI-native practice model in the US. Australian regulatory bodies (LIV, NSW Law Society) have not yet moved on equivalent frameworks — a gap worth monitoring as pressure for access-to-justice solutions grows.
- **MCP as Platform Infrastructure:** The Artificial Lawyer's two MCP-focused pieces this week signal that the connector/integration layer is rapidly becoming the key competitive moat. Whichever platform controls the deepest MCP integrations with core legal systems (DMS, courts, CRM) may define the market architecture for the next several years.
