## Emerging Themes

- **Anthropic enters legal tech directly:** Claude for Word (Beta) marks Anthropic's first deliberate legal market move. Unlike Harvey or CoCounsel, this is a foundational model provider going direct-to-practitioner — a structural threat to the entire legal AI middleware layer. *Australian firms should evaluate Claude for Word against existing tool spend.*

- **The trust gap is the growth constraint:** 83% of legal professionals have AI access but only 22% report high trust. ROI correlates 3× with trust levels. Winning the next wave of legal AI adoption is not a features race — it is a trust and accuracy race. Hallucination incidents (CoCounsel, 729+ citation fabrication cases) are the primary brake on enterprise expansion.

- **European AI arms race with AU implications:** Harvey ($8B, Dublin + Paris) vs Legora ($5.5B) is consuming ~half of global legal tech capital. This concentration is delaying capital availability for APAC-specific solutions and may accelerate inbound moves by both platforms into the Australian market within 12–18 months.

- **Client sentiment diverging from firm sentiment (UK signal):** UK clients want digital, not necessarily AI. Firms racing to deploy AI may be ahead of client appetite, creating reputational risk — especially relevant for Australian firms serving risk-averse corporate clients.

- **Vertical document automation validated:** Avvoka's £14M raise after a decade of bootstrapping confirms investor appetite for AI-native document drafting infrastructure. Comparable AU/APAC opportunities remain underserved.

- **Courts as next legal AI frontier:** Learned Hand and similar startups are targeting court-facing workflows — filings, submissions, hearing prep. This is an underexplored category in Australia given ongoing court modernisation programs.
